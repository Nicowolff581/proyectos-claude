U.S. History · 1861–1877

Civil War & Reconstruction

The Union faces secession and civil war. Emancipation and new amendments open a contested new chapter in citizenship.

Presented by Your Name

1860–1861

Secession and the Confederacy

  • After Lincoln’s election (Nov 1860), South Carolina is the first state to secede.
  • In Feb 1861, seven states form the Confederate States of America. Its constitution protects slavery; Jefferson Davis is president.
  • Four more states join after the fighting starts: 11 Confederate states in total.

1861–1863

A Nation at War

  • April 12, 1861: Confederates fire on Fort Sumter. The war begins.
  • Antietam (1862): about 23,000 casualties, the bloodiest single day in U.S. history.
  • July 1863: Gettysburg (about 51,000 casualties) and Vicksburg turn the tide. The Union controls the Mississippi.

January 1, 1863

The Emancipation Proclamation

  • Declares enslaved people in Confederate-held areas free.
  • It did not cover loyal border states and could not yet be enforced in the South.
  • It turns the war into a fight to end slavery, not only to save the Union.

1863–1865

Lincoln’s Words and Final Days

  • Nov 1863: the Gettysburg Address. About two minutes long, it is one of the most famous American speeches.
  • April 9, 1865: Lee surrenders to Grant at Appomattox Court House.
  • April 14, 1865: John Wilkes Booth shoots Lincoln at Ford’s Theatre, and Andrew Johnson inherits Reconstruction.

1865–1870

Freedom Written into Law

13th1865

Abolishes slavery, except as punishment for a crime.

14th1868

Citizenship for all people born or naturalized in the U.S., with equal protection of the laws.

15th1870

The right to vote cannot be denied because of race, color or previous servitude.

1865–1877

The Promise of Reconstruction

  • The Freedmen’s Bureau (1865) gives food, medical care and schools to freed people.
  • Progress meets violent opposition, and Northern support fades in the 1870s.
  • Compromise of 1877: Hayes becomes president, federal troops leave the South, and Reconstruction ends.

1861–1877

A war that ended slavery, a peace that left equality unfinished.
